<font color="#a5ffc8">(P) Shoshanua Shy – Bringing my son to the police station …, p 17</font>
I do like how the title does a lot for the story, it creates an interesting setting making one wonder where it is when all the focus lays more on the small things around. Which falls into both good and bad, it’s really nice to have such an impactful title, but it would also be nice to have the title reflect more onto what’s going on, and… where is the son?  
  

And, why did you decide to have it be a son who was brought to the station?  
Could it have been a daughter? A friend? A mother? A father?

What changes would you make to the text if there was a change? Same POV character, but a difference in who was brough to the station. _Are there any changes to be made?_

As noted above, you’ve done well with focusing on the small things. Like we’re in the onset of a panic attack and focus lays on grounding to keep control.  
  

One thing I find would do you well would be to include more of the senses, and I encourage you to consider including more elements of the surroundings as well, like with the mention of the windshield. In addition, it would be nice to know where the son is in all of this.

If he’s with her, as the title implies, then it might be worth considering having an interaction between the two. It’s not something you _have_ to do, but it is worth considering. What effect would it have on the text and is it an effect you’d like?

Still, you’ve done good and it’s a nice little poem, pretty details that paint a lovely picture.

Looking forward to what more you may do ᵔᵜᵔ  
Keep on writing! You got this!


<font color="#a5ffc8">(R)   Jim Crace – The prospect from the silver hills, p 189</font>
Sweet sorrow, unforgiving embrace

A lovely little story where the lonely takes place

The slow decent into insanity one sees, first just stories to keep oneself sane, slowly being what turns your mind a mess. _What do you see, when you can’t see reality?_

Your text is very thought-provoking, and your writing is good at building images and evoking feelings and understanding. "Sometimes he wrapped his arms around boulders, warmed by the sun, and embraced them. My wife, he said. He kissed boulders." Concise and clear, sweet and sad, the lonely shining bright.

The story progressing is also good, starting with folks just indulging someone who needs company, slowly expanding to them wanting to keep away and ending with forces trying to get him back. Alas, _it’s too late_.

The ending is very fun, sounding happy though the eyes of him  
But all other see a tragedy

It’s very pretty, well done!!

For feedback, I find that it was an interesting choice to not use “” or a dash to indicate

speech, giving a bit of a challenge to what is fully being said between the characters. It’s not too hard to decipher, but it did give a double take in some parts.

The feedback we've gotten has been very kind and sweet, something I enjoy the most is hearing how the text was interpreted and what feelings it might have evoked in the reader. It's delightful to know the strengths and weaknesses of the text, so that one can take that in mind in revisions and in creating new works.

I like knowing my weakness so that I can build it up to a strength.

Hearing interpretations is also really helpful in understanding how our writing conveys information, and how that effects the reading experience. For, even if the wrong understanding is reached, it's very useful information if one every wants to reach that understanding the text evoked.

Sometimes one might have a thought in mind for why the writing should be as it is, but if the reader finds it distracting and that was *not* the intent, then it's good to know as to ensure the same mistake is not made again.
