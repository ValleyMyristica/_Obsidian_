2. **Your development as feedback-giver/peer reviewer**

What I find most important when giving feedback is to make sure to encourage the writer to keep writing. That is priority number one, cause, *how are you to improve is you stop creating?*

It's so important, and I truly do hope I'm able to bring encouragement


The second is to help the writer understand how the reader read and understood the text. With all the different feedbacks, it's clear that there are different understandings. Telling of your own understanding and parts that stood out is nice as it lets one know how the text "functions" in a way. 

Giving advice is also quite important, but if you misunderstood the text, or the authors intent, then it might not be the most useful advice. Therefore I find it better to tell of what conclusion one reached as well as what might help improve the text.

Oftentimes, the texts aren't bad, it more feels like they need a little extra push, a little extra focus
Pinpointing exact places of improvement and being clear in *why* we recommend doing these changes feel good. Cause, even if that's not what the author wants for *this* story, it could still be useful advice applicable for another story.

Like, what if I *want* things to feel as if they're bleeding together and everything feels floaty and off?
You never really know, so it feels important to be clear what effect implementing the feedback would have on the text.

In other words
I write a lot for my feedback and I do try to give useful advice
One can only hope that it's of use


3. **Your development as a writer**

I do find I've gotten somewhat better at implementing the senses, I'm far more aware, trying to find ways to include *taste* as I find that to be the most evocative one if you're able to get it right.

It's also been really helpful in getting feedback, it's clear one makes mistakes, and we're working on improving them. Everything is a first draft in a sense, so there is nothing wrong with making mistakes, but you want to create something good so that the feedback you get can truly focus in on what the text truly lack.

To this point, sometimes one tries something "experimental" and the feedback is useful in gauging how worthwhile it was. For some it can click, while for others, it leaves them confused. It's important to find a balance, break as many rules you can while still having it familiar enough to be understood
