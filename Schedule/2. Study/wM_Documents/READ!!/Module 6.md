With that in mind, I'd like to give some advice as to how to proceed when you've gotten a first draft down. The first step is to make it, after that you can make it good.

* Read though the text and pick out what you like
{ Most importantly, try and find a "theme" or "narrative" something that can be the base for what you're creating. } { It's important in the sense that it gives you something to fall back on when you're unsure where to go, as well as give the story a more cohesive feel. To have some sort of impact, you have to have focus. Everything should be presented though the lens of that theme. Emphasize it. }


For this text, there seems to be some sort of theme of loneliness and how that makes you feel like trash. *Leen into that.*

When talking about the senior, try and bring up a contrast of how they'll happily make food for one another to enjoy. While the POV character is simply doing it to have something to eat cause their body demands it. Maybe the food they're making is just something frozen they're heating up, they have no care. Throwing the box in the garbage, or trying to, the box is too big and the garbage is to full. Putting it in a corner, not that it matters, it just matches the rest of the apartment.

(( Something along those lines. Don't just 'tell' us the story, show us though actions and how the characters interact with the environment. What does those interactions tell us about the character? How does it connect to the "theme"? ))

The fun thing about writing is that a few words can do a lot of things at once. It can tell simply what's going on, inform the reader of what the character is like as well as highlight the theme. Not every sentence needs to be golden and do everything, but try and keep it in the back of your mind. Try to always have a theme to fall back on, it makes the whole text feel more cohesive, instead of a trailing thought of sitting and looking out the window.


Though, if you *want* the text to just flow like this does, there can be a purpose to that to. Like for a memory, or a dream, things that feel less tangential, that is where having more "floaty" text is fitting. Still, then one should lean into it.
